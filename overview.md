- This week we got some firsthand experience of the simultaneous benefits and drawbacks of digital technologies for fieldwork. Despite having done the readings (and skimmed the Billings Estate Virtual Exhibit), I immediately started having problems filling out the monument data sheet. the first grave I looked at did not fit the standard criteria for identifying headstone shapes. I thought about how it would be much easier to just draw the headstone, and then as soon as the thought was fully formed in my mind I saw what we had been talking about in class. Drawing is something I have done for a long time, but it requires a specific skill set and practice time dedicated to honing that skill set. I'm sure many of my classmates would not agree that drawing would be the easiest way to capture the data. It's also not as useful in a dataset; at the very least it requires further manipulation offsite.
- once i started working my way through the form to the best of my ability, I started thinking about the benefits of teamwork; how it would take one person a very long time to survey even a cemetery like the Billings Estate which has relatively few headstones, but the group of us made it faster.
- I also started thinking about how i wasn't sure what i should be looking for on the stones. obviously i had the form questions to guide me, but when something didn't fit into one option i had to decide which category it fit in better. which option would be more significant? The photo i took for the kobotools form didn't save, I'm going to see if i can find a photo online maybe. the text panel and the top of the grave were shaped like a flower. maybe a scholar of the time period would know immediately that this motif is specific to a particular maker and a particular year, but which of my almost-right options will relay to them this information?
- I thought about how ideally we would collect every possible data point about everything, how quickly that can get out of hand and become an impossible task, but how technology can help with that if we use it consciously and intentionally.
- I thought about how drawing the grave would make me look at it as a whole object, but how after the second grave i was just looking at them in individual parts depending what the form asked: is there a base? what shape is the top? what direction is it facing?
- i was always careful and respectful, i watched where i walked, i didn't touch anything, but i realized i still wasnt totally thinking about them as people's graves. I wasn't thinking about the people as people. and that matters! even though my actions were appropriate, it matters! and i will pay attention to that moving forwards. they are always people first.
- Similarly, when we were discussing the technique for plotting a site map by hand, I couldn't help but feel a bit silly that I had thought we would just be making our best hand-drawn approximations of the cemetery. I realized quickly that even though i might have misunderstood this specific project, there is still undeniable value in a hand-drawn sitemap even without calculations. They simply have different applications. they also compliment eachother quite nicely.
